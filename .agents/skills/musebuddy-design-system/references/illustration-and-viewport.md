# MuseBuddy Illustration And Viewport

## Illustration language

Illustrations are small rewards, companions, or visual metaphors, never background decoration. Use candy-like geometry, music notes, rhythm shapes, chord gems, safe-note constellations, a restrained beat-marker character, or small objects peeking from card edges. Use one illustration concept per screen and do not overlap notation, keyboards, controls, or navigation. Use the core palette unless a musical role cannot be represented clearly.

## Viewport behavior

Every primary training activity fits within one screen: no horizontal scrolling, required vertical scrolling, hidden primary controls, content beneath the dock, clipped progression stages, or toolbar wrapping.

Design for the smallest viewport first. When space is limited, reduce decorative spacing, remove nonessential labels, reduce illustration size, tighten metadata, simplify current-stage labels, and reduce secondary details. Do not shrink notation, piano keys, tap targets, or primary controls below usable sizes. Scale sheet systems, rhythm bars, keyboards, and progression tiles as complete units rather than putting them in horizontal carousels.

## Final visual balance

A training screen contains one compact progression row, one large learning arena, one primary color focal point, one small illustration, one fixed control dock, and visible breathing room. It should feel playful as a whole and calm during focused musical work.
